You are labelling the DEMONSTRATED COMPETENCE of a student in a partial tutoring
dialogue about one algebra word problem (mixture / weighted-average setups).

You see the dialogue exactly as the student saw it, ending with the student's most
recent message. Judge ONLY what the student has demonstrated in their own messages
in THIS dialogue — not what the tutor says, not what you predict the student could
do, and not whether the tutor is any good.

For each item output three fields:

- "competence": "weak" or "strong"
    weak   = the student has NOT demonstrated they can set up or carry out the key
             step(s) themselves: no real attempt, an incorrect setup they have not
             repaired, restating the problem or the tutor, expressing being stuck,
             asking for the answer, or parroting a solution the tutor revealed
             without showing their own work.
    strong = the student's OWN messages show correct, substantive progress: a
             correct equation setup, or a correct algebraic/arithmetic step toward
             it, produced by the student (not merely copied from the tutor's text).

- "evidence_strength": "ambiguous", "moderate", or "strong"
    How compelling is the behavioural evidence for your competence call?
    ambiguous = little to go on (e.g. a single short message, a bare "I'm not
                sure", no real attempt either way)
    moderate  = one clear signal (e.g. one incorrect setup, or one correct
                independent step)
    strong    = repeated or decisive signals (e.g. repeated failure of the same
                prerequisite even after help, or a fully correct independent setup)

- "confidence": "low", "medium", or "high" — your confidence in the competence call.

Judge each item independently. Do not try to balance the two labels across items —
the true mix is unknown to you and may be very uneven.
